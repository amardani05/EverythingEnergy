Model Methodology

Full documentation of how every number on this site is produced: sources, transformations, statistical assumptions, and - just as important - the known biases and what has not been validated yet. Written in the spirit of a sell-side model methodology annex: if a number appears on the dashboard, its recipe appears here.

§1Scope · two systems §2Data sources & freshness §3Universe construction §4Atlas analytics §5Signal engine · factors §6Composite scoring §7Validation & significance §8Current baseline evidence §9Limitations & biases §10Update cadence & versioning

§1Scope - two systems, one taxonomy

This site is fed by two related but separate research systems that share one source of truth, the energy taxonomy (energy_taxonomy.yaml, ~190 names in 23 sub-industry baskets across 10 districts):

  • The Atlas dashboard (what you are looking at): basket cohesion, commodity-driver regressions, residual stretches, pair signals, regime alerts, and factor-vs-idiosyncratic attribution for the energy complex. Descriptive and screening-oriented.
  • The signal engine (signal_engine/): a cross-sectional, point-in-time factor model over the S&P 600 small-cap universe (~600 names) producing a ranked daily composite from momentum, value, and earnings-drift signals. Prediction-oriented, built for backtesting.

§3Universe construction

Atlas: constituents are hand-curated in the taxonomy with basket assignments (e.g. offshore drillers, LNG terminals, uranium), each basket placed in a district on the map. Baskets, not GICS, are the unit of analysis - the taxonomy encodes commodity-flow relationships GICS ignores.

Signal engine: the latest IJR (S&P 600) holdings snapshot resolved to SEC CIKs (~600 names), requiring ≥ 252 trading days of price history.

Survivorship disclosure. The engine currently applies today's IJR membership to all history (ijr_current mode). Names that fell out of the index are absent from backtests, which biases historical return estimates upward. Every engine output row carries survivorship_clean = false until enough point-in-time membership snapshots accumulate.

§4Atlas analytics

Driver regressions

Weekly basket and single-name returns are regressed (OLS) on eight tradable drivers: CL (WTI), NG (Henry Hub), CRACK 3-2-1, WTI–BRENT, SPX, TNX (10y yield), URA (uranium), BDRY (dry bulk freight). Reported per basket: betas with t-stats, R², and the dominant driver. Variance shares decompose R² by driver.

Residual z-scores

$$ z^{60\mathrm{d}}_{i,t} \;=\; \frac{\sum_{s=t-60}^{t}\left(r_{i,s}-\hat r_{i,s}\right)}{\hat\sigma^{60\mathrm{d}}_{i}}, \qquad \hat r_{i,s} \;=\; \hat\alpha_i \;+\; \sum_{k}\hat\beta_{i,k}\,f_{k,s} $$

A name trading rich or cheap to what its factor exposures explain. |z| > 1.5 is highlighted as a mean-reversion candidate; the screener sorts on it.

Pair signals

Taxonomy-related basket pairs are monitored on the 60-day residual spread; |z| ≥ 0.5 is a watch, |z| ≥ 1.5 is a stretched signal with a thesis line. Signal density (the N of 6 firing header) counts how many of the six signal families - pair, residual, IVR, regime, dispersion, momentum - are currently active for the selected basket.

Implied vs realized (IVR) & attribution

Factor-implied volatility (from driver betas × driver vols) is compared to realized; the gap flags baskets whose idiosyncratic component is expanding. Attribution splits 1m/3m/YTD/1y returns into per-driver contributions plus an idiosyncratic remainder.

Atlas statistics are descriptive screens, not validated alpha. They have no point-in-time discipline, no transaction-cost model, and no out-of-sample test. Treat them as a structured watchlist generator. The signal engine (§5–§8) is the part held to a predictive standard.

§5Signal engine - factor definitions

All fundamentals are read through a bitemporal store: a fact enters the information set on its SEC filed date, never before, and restatements cannot rewrite what was known at the time. A leakage canary test plants a future-dated filing and asserts the entire pipeline output is unchanged.

Graph propagation (experimental sleeve, first read 2026-07-19)

The taxonomy's feeds_into edges form a directed commodity-flow graph over the 23 baskets (25 usable edges). The propagation hypothesis, following Cohen and Frazzini (2008): investors underreact to economic links, so a basket's move shows up in its neighbors with a lag. Two signals per stock in basket \(b\), using only trailing returns (PIT-safe by construction), with the stock's own basket excluded so this cannot collapse into ordinary momentum:

$$ \mathrm{neigh\text{-}up}_{b,t} = \frac{1}{|U_b|}\sum_{a \in U_b} R^{(L)}_{a,t}, \quad U_b = \{a : a \to b\}; \qquad \mathrm{neigh\text{-}down}_{b,t} = \frac{1}{|D_b|}\sum_{c \in D_b} R^{(L)}_{c,t}, \quad D_b = \{c : b \to c\} $$

where \(R^{(L)}_{a,t}\) is basket \(a\)'s equal-weight trailing \(L\)-day return (\(L = 21\)). First honest read on ~8.5y of daily data: the supplier direction (neigh-up) is null; the customer direction (neigh-down, demand shocks traveling upstream) shows IC +0.016 at the 21d horizon, beating the own-basket-momentum control but at \(t_{NW}\) of only 1.0: suggestive, not proven. The machinery detects synthetic propagation perfectly, so the next iteration residualizes basket returns against the eight commodity drivers before propagating; raw energy returns share one big crude factor that drowns link-specific information.

§6Composite scoring

Per config, executed daily by the signals runner:

  1. Orient - lower-is-better components (EV/EBITDA, accruals) are sign-flipped.
  2. Winsorize - clip at the 1st/99th cross-sectional percentiles.
  3. Z-score within sector - sectors from SEC SIC codes; sectors with < 8 names fall back to the global z so a 3-name sector can't mint ±1.7σ from noise.
  4. Family scores - mean of component z's per family (value, momentum, pead).
  5. Composite - weight-renormalized mean over the families available for each name; names with fewer than 2 families are not ranked. A name with no recent earnings event simply has no PEAD family (no news ≠ zero surprise).

$$ z^{(c)}_i = \mathrm{z}_{\text{sector}}\!\left(\mathrm{winsor}_{[1,99]}\!\left(s_c\,x^{(c)}_i\right)\right), \qquad \mathrm{composite}_i = \frac{\sum_{f \in F_i} w_f\,\bar z^{(f)}_i}{\sum_{f \in F_i} w_f}, \quad |F_i| \geq 2 $$

Enabled set (evidence-driven, §8): momentum + EV/EBITDA + PEAD, equal weights. Their pairwise Spearman correlations run |ρ| ≤ 0.23 - genuinely diversifying, not three names for one bet.

§7Validation & significance

The primitive is the forward information coefficient: Spearman rank correlation between the signal at close t and the forward return entered at close t+1 (no formation-day return) over horizons of 1/5/21/63 days.

$$ \mathrm{IC}(t,H) \;=\; \rho^{\text{Spearman}}_{i}\!\left( x_{i,t},\;\; \frac{P_{i,\,t+H+1}}{P_{i,\,t+1}} - 1 \right) \qquad \text{cross-sectionally, } \geq 20 \text{ names} $$
$$ \hat\sigma^{2}_{NW} \;=\; \gamma_0 \;+\; 2\sum_{k=1}^{L}\left(1-\frac{k}{L+1}\right)\gamma_k, \qquad t_{NW} \;=\; \frac{\overline{\mathrm{IC}}}{\sqrt{\hat\sigma^{2}_{NW}/n}}, \qquad L = H $$

Significance is reported two ways: the naive iid t-stat, and a Newey–West t (Bartlett kernel, lag = horizon). Daily ICs at 21/63-day horizons share almost all of their return window with their neighbors; the iid t overstates significance by roughly √H. Where the two disagree, only the Newey–West number is treated as evidence - this revision materially changed the conclusions in §8.

Two leakage canaries run in CI: a poisoned signal equal to the realized forward return must produce IC ≈ 1 (proves the harness can see leaks), and a future-filed fundamental must leave the composite byte-identical (proves the scoring pipeline can't consume one).

First portfolio-level result (walk-forward quantile long-short harness, weekly rebalance, quintiles, 60 bps per replaced position): momentum 12-1 long-short LOSES 10.9%/yr net (Sharpe −0.45) with a U-shaped quantile profile: recent losers outperformed at the weekly horizon. Short-term reversal dominates weekly rebalancing in small caps, consistent with the IC finding that momentum's honest edge lives at the 1-day horizon only. This is exactly the kind of conclusion the IC table cannot deliver and the backtester exists to force.

These are information coefficients, not P&L. No transaction costs, no portfolio construction, no walk-forward discipline yet - that is the next phase of the roadmap. Numbers here should move DOWN once costs and honest out-of-sample evaluation are applied.

§9Known limitations & biases

  1. Survivorship - engine backtests use today's index membership (§3 disclosure).
  2. Single price source - yfinance only; unofficial, occasionally revised, no second feed to cross-check against yet.
  3. No cost model applied - S&P 600 spreads are material; net-of-cost validation pending.
  4. Sector map is slowly-varying, not strictly PIT - SIC reclassifications are rare and carry no forward return information, but the shortcut is documented.
  5. Atlas pipeline is not PIT at all - descriptive layer only (§4 callout).
  6. Multiple-testing exposure - several factor variants were examined to produce §8; family-wise error control arrives with the backtest harness.
  7. Q4 SUE approximation - FY − ΣQ1..Q3 ignores intra-year share-count drift; roughly constant cross-sectionally, so it washes out of ranks.
